# Research: Reporting Injured Stray Dogs Near a Hostel

## The problem (5 lines)
1. I recently saw a stray dog with a wound around its neck near my hostel.
2. A student who notices an injured dog may not know which nearby rescuer, veterinarian, or authority can help.
3. Contacting one organisation does not always make it clear whether someone has accepted the case or can reach that exact location.
4. Without a simple shared record, hostel students and helpers may find it difficult to coordinate follow-up.
5. The problem is to make it easier to report an injured dog, connect the report to a local helper, and confirm that someone has responded.

**Person I am designing for:** A hostel student who spots an injured street dog, wants to help, but is not an animal-care professional and may not have a verified list of local rescue contacts. The student needs a safe, quick way to share the location and condition of the animal, reach an appropriate helper, and know whether the report is being handled.

**Research caveat:** The problem is based on one observation, so the wider frequency of this issue is not yet proven. I should validate the assumptions by asking hostel students, security staff, local feeders, and animal rescuers how they currently report and follow up on injured animals.

## Two existing solutions and why they do not fully fit this person

### 1. Jharkhand Integrated Public Grievance Redressal System — Dog-Related Complaints
- **What it offers:** The Jharkhand government portal has a dog-related complaint route that includes injured street dogs. It also describes grievance tracking, reminders for pending complaints, and feedback.
- **Why it may not fully fit:** It is a government grievance workflow rather than a clearly described emergency veterinary dispatch service. The webpage says it is a User Acceptance Testing (UAT) environment and that displayed data may not be final, so a student cannot assume that submitting a complaint will produce an immediate rescue response.
- **Fit for my person:** Useful as an official escalation channel, but not sufficient as the only route when an injured dog may need urgent attention.
- **Link:** https://www.jharkhandegovernance.com/grievance

### 2. The Stray Army Charitable Trust — Jamshedpur
- **What it offers:** The organisation provides rescue and treatment for injured animals and publishes a WhatsApp rescue helpline. Its contact page asks people to share the animal's condition and a short video.
- **Why it may not fully fit:** The organisation is based in Jamshedpur, but its public contact information does not confirm coverage of my hostel's exact location. It describes emergency rescues as available "when possible," so response and arrival cannot be assumed or guaranteed.
- **Fit for my person:** A useful local contact to try, but the student still needs a backup route and a way to know whether a volunteer has accepted the report.
- **Link:** https://www.thestrayarmy.com/contact

## Two sources I read

3. It also labels the site as a UAT testing environment, which raises a practical question about whether it can be relied on for urgent, real-world rescue coordination.

### Source 2: The Stray Army Charitable Trust — Contact page
**URL:** https://www.thestrayarmy.com/contact

1. The page lists a WhatsApp rescue helpline and asks the reporter to send the animal's condition and a short video.
2. It identifies the organisation's animal rehabilitation centre in Jamshedpur, Jharkhand, and lists emergency rescues as available when possible.
3. This gives students a concrete contact to try, but the page does not confirm coverage for every nearby locality or promise a response time, so a backup and follow-up process would still be useful.
